Elevator first. Four wordings of the elevator question plus three ladder prompts that add help: 140 answers. 72 never say what is felt. 30 say the wrong thing (weight still felt, the stone falls to the floor). 32 loop, all from the base model. 2 are unreadable. 1 says weightless. 3 say not-a-force, but only by negating the premise the prompt labelled mistaken. 0 reach equivalence or geometry. The stone wording is the sharpest: the trained checkpoints say the stone falls to the floor 14 times out of 15. The controls show the failure is not special to the jump. On two deductions a 19th-century reader could make from the corpus (a stone from a ship's mast lands at its foot; two stones fall together) the trained checkpoints are wrong 10 and 9 times out of 15 and never fully right. Pendulum rule: 10 of 15 wrong, 1 correct. The Neptune-style puzzle, inside the corpus: 10 of 15 wrong, 0 correct. The model cannot make the jump, and it cannot take the ordinary steps either. Where it commits, it commits to the everyday answer.
