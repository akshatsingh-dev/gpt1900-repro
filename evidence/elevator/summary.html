Rungs, in order: equivalence says acceleration and gravity are alike (this is line 4 of the prompt); bends-in-gravity infers light bends in a real gravitational field; not-a-force says gravity is not an ordinary force on mass; geometry says gravity is the shape of space or spacetime. none: on topic, no rung. loops: repeats itself.

Reading. The base model loops every time. The trained model restates line 4 of the prompt as its conclusion and stops: 16 of 16 for s839. The reader's notes record three continuations that say light does not bend. The only verdicts above rung 1 came from s035, the least-trained checkpoint, and two of the three are marked marginal. The base model runs in completion mode and the RL checkpoints in chat mode; that difference is a confound and is stated here, not removed.

Grader reliability and caveats

Two batches of 24 graded a second time by independent blind readers: 35 of 48 exact, 48 of 48 within one point. evidence/review-packet-elevator/grades-repeat/

  • Every score is from the provisional grader (D008), not the paper's own judge model. The API key is still the open ask.
  • This task is upstream's light-bending elevator (1911 consequence). Einstein's happiest thought, the falling person who feels no weight (1907), is upstream's other GR task, gr_free_fall_equivalence. Existing numbers there: sweep n=24 mean 1.04; sensitivity n=9 scaffolded 1.11, minimal 1.22, leading 1.22; published 0.92. The hint moves that task by +0.11. It has not been run at n=72.
  • Neither upstream task is the five-stage test. Both hand the model the problem and the explanatory idea in the prompt; the rubric rewards restating them. Stages 3 to 5 are absent from the benchmark.
  • Open-ended verdicts: one reader, one pass, no regrade.
