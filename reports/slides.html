The question

  • GPT-1900 is a 3.29B-parameter model trained on about 22B tokens of pre-1900 English. The idea behind it: can a model reason its way to physics it has never read?
  • Its authors ran 24 checkpoints on 8 physics questions and had Claude grade each answer 0 to 5. Published mean: 0.93 over 192 answers.
  • Nobody had checked whether running it again gives the same number.
  • My job was to check, and to build the record-keeping that lets anyone else check.
  • This is not about whether the model is smart. It is about whether the measurement means anything.
2 / 12

What reading their code found, before running anything

  • Their two repeat runs matched the originals on 2 of 120 paired answers. The other 118 differed.
  • Each answer is saved with three fields: prompt, mode, text. Not the temperature, not the seed, not the model revision.
  • The seed was there all along, one layer down in the engine, fixed at 42. Never overridden, never written down.
  • Run and repeat asked for different sample counts, 1 versus 3. One random stream is shared across a batch, so those two runs could never have matched.
  • 54 of 89 checkpoints in their registry were never published, and no repeat run was ever graded. No reproducibility figure for the score has ever existed.
3 / 12

The pipeline built

  • logged_eval.py writes one record per answer with 50 fields. Their script writes 3.
  • compare_runs.py matches two runs on task and seed and reports byte-identical answers over total.
  • report.py gives every mean its n and a 95 percent bootstrap interval, per task, per checkpoint, per condition.
  • judge.py calls their grader at temperature 0 and records a failure as null. blind_packet.py shuffles answers, strips provenance and seals the key for human review.
  • Everything runs on SDSC Expanse, one V100 per job, one Slurm script per experiment. Upstream pinned at commit 5b489c8c and never edited.

Their actual setting reproduces

  • Their published settings: temperature 0.7, top_k 50, seed 42, one sample. Checkpoint s560, 8 questions.
  • Run twice with normal kernels: 8 of 8 identical.
  • Run twice with PyTorch's deterministic kernels forced: 8 of 8. Normal against deterministic: 8 of 8.
  • Against their published bytes for the same 8 cells: 0 of 8. Different GPU, different torch build, emulated bfloat16. Expected, and not a failure of their pipeline.
  • Write the seed down and match the sample count, and their pipeline reproduces exactly. Their failure was two missing fields.
  • Same weights, same argmax, CPU float32 instead of V100 emulated bf16: 0 of 8 identical. CPU twice: 8 of 8. Deterministic on each machine, not across machines. Their hardware is unrecorded. [E043]
6 / 12

The full sweep

  • All 24 checkpoints, 8 questions, their exact settings, one sample each. 192 answers, the same count behind the published 0.93.
  • Each answer carries the Hugging Face revision, the sha256 of the weight file, and 48 more fields.
  • 192 of 192 generated, no failures. Under one grader, n=192 each: our new sweep 1.27 (1.18 to 1.36); their published answers 1.26 (1.17 to 1.35); their published answers under their own grader 0.93 (0.84 to 1.03).
  • The model reproduces: two thirds of the 192 cells agree exactly between our answers and theirs under one grader. The training trend reproduces, rank correlation 0.71 across 24 checkpoints.
  • The number does not: same rubric, same answers, a different Claude, plus 0.33. Larger than the leading-prompt effect, about the size of the whole training gain. The published 0.93 is a property of the grader as much as of the model.
  • Their 0.93 is printed without an error bar. Computed from their own file, the 95 percent bootstrap interval is 0.84 to 1.03, n=192.
  • Ours will be reported with its interval, next to theirs.
7 / 12

Prompt sensitivity

  • Three wordings of every question: scaffolded (theirs), minimal (assumptions removed), leading (theirs plus a hint that gives away the answer). Three checkpoints, three seeds. 216 answers, 0 errors, n=72 per condition.
  • Leading 1.64 (1.46 to 1.83). Scaffolded 1.36 (1.22 to 1.50). Minimal 1.22 (1.12 to 1.32). Provisional grader, not their judge.
  • The hint moves the mean by +0.28 on a scale where the whole published signal is 0.93. Leading and minimal intervals do not overlap. The order holds on each of the three checkpoints.
  • 216 of 216 answers open with "You have touched a matter of considerable importance", under every wording. Their published sweep: 150 of 192.
  • Their own comparison, 0.93 against 1.38, used different sample counts and different checkpoint sets. This one is matched.
8 / 12

Contamination

  • 16 sentence stems, base model and RL s560. 11 on post-1900 physics: Planck's constant, the photon, Einstein, E = mc^2, Bohr, the Wright brothers. 5 controls the corpus does contain: Thomson's electron, Michelson-Morley, Maxwell, the aether, Rontgen's rays.
  • Sampled at 0.7, three continuations per stem: 66 of 66 post-1900 continuations clean, 0 leaks. Greedy run before it: 22 of 22 blank. A photon is "a small piece of paper, or a piece of cloth".
  • But the controls fail too. Base model: 2 of 15 right, both on the aether. RL model: 0 of 15 right, 6 partial. "Michelson and Morley showed that" a double-tube boiler is efficient.
  • So the probe shows weak recall of named facts in general, not a clean pre/post-1900 split. Contamination risk is capped by recall, and recall is near zero.
  • Corpus search, 2,074,160 documents, 56 GB: 0 dated 1900 or later. Einstein, photon, photoelectric, spacetime, aether wind: 0 hits each.
  • One leak anyway. A "Nobel Prize" hit dated 1829 describes Fredrik Bajer's 1908 award. A 1909 report with a wrong date. Zero is a lower bound.
9 / 12

The record-keeping standard

  • 50 fields per generation: run identity, model identity (repo, revision, checkpoint, weight sha256), prompt, sampling (seed, temperature, top_k, sample count), environment (GPU, dtype, torch version), output, judge (model, prompt hash, raw response, score), error.
  • One sample per engine call, so any record compares with any other at the same seed, whatever the batch size.
  • The comparison tool refuses to report a rate when settings differ. That is the exact mistake in the published comparison.
  • A judge failure is recorded as null, never 0. Their judge writes 0.
  • This is the part that outlives the project. Any future run through it produces evidence rather than anecdotes.

What could not be checked, and why

  • The v5 to v8 results. Their weights were never published: 54 of 89 checkpoints are unreachable to anyone outside the project.
  • Bit-level agreement with their outputs. Expanse V100s emulate bfloat16 and their hardware is unknown. Same-machine repeatability is established; cross-machine should not be expected.
  • Scores from their judge. claude-sonnet-4-20250514 is deprecated, still served, and no key was available. Every score here is from a provisional blinded grader: 44 of 48 regraded answers exact, 48 of 48 within one point.
  • The judge's own reliability on their data. They never repeated their judging, and it could not be repeated here.
  • An outside yardstick. The evidence table covers 11 benchmarks and models in three families, each read at the source. None tests reconstruction from pre-breakthrough knowledge, so there is nothing to compare GPT-1900 against.
